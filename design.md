# OCP's (Onchain Club Protocol) Design

## What is a DAO? 

While a traditional firm prefers a top-down power structure that relies heavily on bureaucracy that is both subjective and centralized, DAOs offer an alternative mechanism for achieving consensus within an organization: rule enforcement via smart contracts. 

In a DAO, everything is subject to voting: hiring, strategy, development, and the budget of their treasury being some. All proposals - the mechanism through which people vote - are publicly discussed and voted on. What differentiates DAOs from a traditional firm isn't only the fact that the votes are public, but also that the methods of voting can widely range from *quadratic voting* (one token grants one vote, but two tokens grant four votes) to *liquid democracy* (representatives are elected to making voting decisions, but their role as a representative can be revoked at any time).

## How does OCP design it's DAO structure? 

At the core of OCP's design is *Standing*: nontransferable tokens that represent participation in a club, gradually decaying overtime. These tokens cannot be bought, sold, transferred, or delegated - they are merely gained by being physically present at club functioning. Reputation generally quantifies a persons voting power in the club, while also acting as a generally accurate method to sorting the most active to least active members in the club. 

The provisional decay half-life is *x* days: without new participation exceeding decay, *x* rep becomes *x* rep after *x* days and *x* after *x* days. This encourages continuous participation, ensuring that pure seniority does not dominate the club.
